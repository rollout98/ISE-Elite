# ISE Elite V7.9 — Full-Session Scalp Engine Architecture

Status: research-only design. No live authority, no production promotion, no merge authorization implied.

## Objective

Design a prop-compatible MNQ scalp engine that increases the number of independent, qualified intraday opportunities without turning ISE into a rapid-fire/HFT system. The target is to determine whether a 17:00 CT prior-day to 15:00 CT current-day research window can repeatedly expose enough realistic, capturable opportunity to support a $500–$1,000 daily objective on appropriate market days while preserving strict drawdown and trade-density controls.

The daily-dollar objective is a research target for available and capturable opportunity, not a quota that forces trades.

## Core design principle

ISE V7.9 does not take every price fluctuation. It takes every *new qualified micro-opportunity* that passes higher-timeframe context, 1-minute trigger quality, lifecycle separation, and compliance/governance checks.

The engine is intentionally layered:

- 5-minute: directional context, trend/range state, exhaustion, and post-entry runner authority.
- 3-minute: structural context, local regime confirmation, compression/expansion structure.
- 1-minute: primary scalp opportunity detection and execution timing.
- Sub-minute: deferred to market replay/live telemetry only; historical 1-minute data must not claim exact 30-second holding behavior.

## Trading window

Research session: 17:00 CT previous day through 15:00 CT current day.

Initial attribution zones are descriptive, not hard entry filters:

1. Evening reopen / early Asia: 17:00–19:00.
2. Asia development: 19:00–23:00.
3. Overnight transition: 23:00–03:00.
4. Europe/London-to-US premarket: 03:00–06:30.
5. US pre-open/open development: 06:30–08:45.
6. NY transition window: 08:45–09:05.
7. NY secondary pullback/transition: 09:05–10:00.
8. Late morning: 10:00–12:00.
9. Midday/early afternoon: 12:00–15:00.

The study must report results by zone before any zone is enabled/disabled.

## Initial position model

Instrument: MNQ.

Research size: 3 contracts.

The three-contract model is designed as a hybrid scalp rather than a fixed all-out exit:

- Contract 1: first scalp objective, initially studied at +30 ticks.
- Contract 2: second scalp objective, initially studied at +40 or +50 ticks.
- Contract 3: earned runner only when continuation authority is present; otherwise exit with the scalp lifecycle.

Parallel comparison must also report simple all-out targets at 30, 40, and 50 ticks so the runner cannot hide a weak base scalp edge.

No scale-in during V7.9 Phase 1.

## Opportunity families

### 1. Trend impulse / continuation

Purpose: participate in a new short-horizon directional expansion while aligned with 3-minute/5-minute context.

Candidate ingredients:

- 5-minute directional state is aligned or strengthening.
- 3-minute structure is not opposing the direction.
- 1-minute displacement exceeds recent local noise.
- Directional efficiency improves versus the preceding micro-window.
- Entry is not excessively extended from the most recent reset/reference structure.
- Recent 1-minute bars do not show immediate exhaustion/rejection.

### 2. Pullback / reset continuation

Purpose: re-enter an established move after a genuine reset rather than chase the original impulse.

Candidate ingredients:

- Higher-timeframe direction remains intact.
- A pullback/reset is structurally observable.
- The reset matures before re-entry; immediate same-state re-fire is suppressed.
- Reclaim/resumption occurs on the 1-minute stream.
- The new opportunity must receive a new lifecycle/opportunity ID.

This family should reuse the causal reset-maturity lessons from V7.8.x, but its thresholds are not assumed to be identical until tested in the full-session scalp dataset.

### 3. Directional transition / reversal

Purpose: capture a real state change, including the type of NY transition previously observed around 08:45–09:05 CT.

Candidate ingredients:

- Prior directional efficiency deteriorates.
- Opposite-side structure is reclaimed/accepted.
- Acceleration shifts in the new direction.
- 3-minute context stops confirming the old direction.
- 1-minute entry is compared directly with delayed 3-minute confirmation to measure entry-price/capture advantage.

Clock time is context only; the strategy may not enter solely because the time is 08:45.

### 4. Mean reversion

Purpose: harvest bounded oscillations only when the market is demonstrably non-trending.

Candidate ingredients:

- 5-minute context is range/compression, not directional trend.
- 3-minute range boundaries/reference mean are stable.
- 1-minute extension reaches a statistically meaningful local extreme.
- Rejection/reclaim occurs before entry.
- Mean-reversion authority is disabled when expansion/trend state activates.

## Micro-opportunity lifecycle

Each opportunity receives a unique ID and transitions through:

1. Observed — conditions begin forming.
2. Armed — higher-timeframe context permits this family.
3. Triggered — causal 1-minute entry condition is met.
4. Entered — research execution fill occurs.
5. Managed — scalp targets, stop, time invalidation, and optional runner are active.
6. Closed — position is flat.
7. Resetting — new entries are blocked until a genuinely new micro-opportunity is observed.
8. Re-armed — a distinct new opportunity can trade.

The lifecycle prevents repeated bars from creating multiple entries from the same underlying move.

## Re-entry and trade-density governor

The compliance governor is independent of alpha generation.

Initial research behavior:

- No simultaneous positions.
- No duplicate entry from the same opportunity ID.
- Minimum one completed 1-minute bar after exit before a new entry is eligible in Phase 1 research.
- A new entry after cooldown still requires a new qualified opportunity; elapsed time alone never re-arms the system.
- Track entries per rolling 5, 15, and 60 minutes.
- Track median time between entries.
- Track repeated-signal suppressions.
- Track hold bars and later, in replay/live telemetry, exact seconds in position.

Hard caps are safety/compliance parameters and must not be optimized to maximize P&L.

Initial reporting caps to simulate, without choosing a winner yet:

- maximum 2 entries per rolling 5 minutes;
- maximum 4 entries per rolling 15 minutes;
- maximum 10 entries per hour;
- maximum 20 entries per research session.

If the raw signal generator routinely hits these caps, the opportunity definition is too noisy and must be improved rather than increasing the caps.

## Entry execution model

Historical Phase 1:

- Signal decision uses completed 1-minute information only.
- Primary execution-realistic fill comparison: next 1-minute bar open.
- Also report idealized signal-close entry strictly as a diagnostic so timing cost is visible.
- Explicit slippage and commission assumptions must be reported separately.

Market Replay Phase 2:

- Capture actual signal timestamp, submit timestamp, acknowledgement, fill timestamp, slippage, and exact hold duration.
- Evaluate whether 30-second-to-few-minute holds occur naturally; do not force a minimum hold if the protective stop is hit.

## Stops and invalidation

V7.9 Phase 1 must compare risk outcomes without optimizing a huge stop grid.

Base stop authority should come from the nearest causal invalidation structure with a hard maximum risk ceiling.

Required diagnostics:

- structural stop distance in ticks;
- MAE before target;
- percentage of trades requiring >30, >50, >75, >100 ticks adverse excursion;
- time-to-target and time-to-stop;
- immediate-failure rate in first 1–3 bars.

A scalp that requires several hundred ticks of adverse tolerance to earn 30–50 ticks is invalid by design.

## Exit model

Phase 1 must test:

- all-out +30 ticks;
- all-out +40 ticks;
- all-out +50 ticks;
- hybrid 30 / 50 / runner;

Runner authority must be earned, not automatic. Initial runner candidate requires:

- first two scalp objectives achieved or equivalent favorable excursion;
- 5-minute direction remains aligned;
- no exhaustion/bias-loss state;
- runner trail becomes active only after sufficient extension.

Post-exit favorable excursion must be measured for every model. If most trades continue substantially after exit, the engine has a capture problem rather than an opportunity problem.

## Timeframe comparison

For each opportunity, produce a paired timing record:

- causal 1-minute trigger timestamp/price;
- first valid 3-minute confirmation timestamp/price;
- first valid 5-minute context confirmation where relevant;
- entry delay in seconds/minutes;
- ticks surrendered while waiting for 3-minute confirmation;
- 1-minute MFE/MAE;
- delayed-entry MFE/MAE;
- whether the 30/40/50-tick target remained available after delay.

This directly answers whether the 1-minute engine is necessary at 08:45 and other high-velocity periods.

## Daily opportunity accounting

V7.9 must distinguish three dollar quantities:

1. Raw market movement — not a valid strategy metric.
2. Qualified available opportunity — MFE associated with causal opportunities that satisfy the research definition.
3. Realized/capturable strategy P&L — what the execution/management model can reasonably capture after stops, sequencing, overlap, slippage, commissions, and governance.

Required daily fields:

- qualified opportunities;
- executed opportunities;
- winners/losses;
- gross realized P&L;
- estimated costs;
- net P&L;
- qualified MFE dollars;
- capture efficiency = net realized opportunity / qualified available opportunity;
- max intraday drawdown;
- maximum consecutive losing trades;
- largest rolling 60-minute loss;
- days >= $300, >= $500, >= $1,000.

## Research sequence

### V7.9.1 — Full-session dataset integrity

Build/verify causal 1-minute MNQ data from 17:00 previous day to 15:00 current day, with contract-aware rollover and enough warmup for derived 3-minute/5-minute state.

### V7.9.2 — Opportunity census

Detect and label all four opportunity families with no trading optimization. Measure count, overlap, MFE/MAE, hour, regime, and family.

### V7.9.3 — 1m vs 3m timing attribution

Pair entries to quantify delay and opportunity lost by waiting for 3-minute confirmation.

### V7.9.4 — Fixed-target scalp replay

Replay 30/40/50-tick all-out exits using identical candidates and execution assumptions.

### V7.9.5 — Hybrid 3-contract lifecycle

Compare 30/50/runner against fixed targets. Runner must not change entry authority.

### V7.9.6 — Compliance/trade-density governor

Apply cooldown, new-opportunity requirement, rolling trade-density telemetry, and firm-specific profile hooks. Measure P&L sacrificed by governance separately from alpha.

### V7.9.7 — Independent walk-forward validation

Freeze the chosen opportunity definition and management model, then validate by untouched time blocks.

### V7.9.8 — NinjaTrader Market Replay shadow test

Measure exact second-level holding times, order timing, slippage, signal spacing, and compliance telemetry before Sim101 authority.

## Promotion gates

Do not promote the scalp engine unless all of the following are demonstrated:

- Positive expectancy after realistic costs across multiple independent periods.
- Daily P&L is not dependent on one hour, one contract month, or a few outlier days.
- Drawdown remains compatible with the intended prop-account risk envelope.
- No evidence that profitability requires rapid-fire duplicate entries.
- The compliance governor removes relatively little genuine edge; if it destroys the strategy, the strategy is not suitable for the intended deployment.
- 1-minute timing advantage survives execution-realistic fills.
- Opportunity/capture analysis shows a credible path toward the daily objective without forced trade count or excessive leverage.

## Relationship to ISE Core

V7.9 is a separate opportunity engine, not a replacement for the frozen ISE Core research track.

ISE Core remains the selective structural strategy. V7.9 is designed to harvest smaller, repeated micro-opportunities. If both survive independent validation, a later portfolio-governance layer will arbitrate conflicts so both engines cannot independently open overlapping positions without a single account-level authority decision.
